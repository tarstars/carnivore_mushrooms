A title with
roots and a refrain.

Research, literary context and an English rendering of the project poem

A real scientific title

The Russian State Library records Flora Khusanovna Fayzieva’s Predatory fungi of Uzbekistan, a 1975 Tashkent dissertation of 159 illustrated pages, with a separate 27-page abstract. We verified its bibliography, not its full text. This project does not establish a species inventory for Uzbekistan.

Two different traps

Arthrobotrys oligospora forms sticky three-dimensional hyphal networks. Drechslerella dactyloides uses rings of three cells that inflate inwards around a passing nematode; research reports rapid inflation in about 0.1–1 second. A living ring is not a rope pulled tight.

The artworks and games juxtapose these mechanisms as a composite fantasy. They are not micrographs, verified Uzbek collection records, or claims of endemic distribution. Studies of prey sensing and olfactory mimicry inform the games without making them calibrated biological models.

A Tashkent-region manufacturer also lists an AN BIO product containing culture liquid and metabolites of A. oligospora. That is a commercial composition statement, not independent evidence of efficacy or local isolation.

When the title became a chorus

A collective Russian-language internet poem built around this title is documented in a compilation dated 28 February 2018. Its first author and complete list of contributors remain unverified. The compiler is not established as the sole author. This is internet verse, not ancient Uzbek folklore.

The new poems and adaptations in this laboratory respond to that collective tradition. They do not reproduce the historical poem.

Rhyme, too, is a hunting ring

English rendering of the original 2026 project poem by Codex.

The catalogue was quiet.
Tashkent. Nineteen seventy-five.
Twenty-seven pages—
and a whole night between the lines.

Someone read the title out.
Another offered it a rhyme.
A third could not shut the window in time:
hyphae were already reaching out of letters.

Predatory fungi of Uzbekistan.

Each line found a voice.
Each voice found a neighbour.
We thought we were writing about a mycelium.
It had been writing through us for years.

Rhyme, too, is a hunting ring.
First you look for a word.
Then it
will not let go.
